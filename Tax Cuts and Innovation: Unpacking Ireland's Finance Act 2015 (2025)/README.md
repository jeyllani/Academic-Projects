## Abstract

<div align="left">
<div style="text-align: justify; max-width: 700px; margin: 0 auto; padding: 10px;">

In 2016, Ireland introduced the Knowledge Development Box (KDB) through the Finance Act 2015, reducing the tax rate on income derived from intellectual property (IP) from 12.5% to 6.25% to foster innovation. This study examines the impact of the KDB on the number of patents filed and on the growth rate of the depreciation of IP assets by Irish firms. Using advanced econometric methodologies, including static and dynamic Difference-in-Differences (DiD) models as well as the Synthetic Control Method (SCM), we assessed the effect of this tax reform.

Our analyses reveal a significant decrease in the number of patents filed and an increase in the depreciation rate of IP assets following the implementation of the KDB. These results suggest that the tax reform may have negatively influenced innovation behaviors and altered the management of IP assets by Irish firms. However, the violation of the parallel trends assumption and the inherent limitations of the available data qualify the robustness of these conclusions, making it difficult to establish a direct causal link between the KDB and the observed changes.

This study highlights the complexity of interactions between tax policies and innovation. While the reform appears to have impacted the number of patents filed and the management of IP assets, it is essential to consider the methodological challenges and data limitations in future studies to further explore these relationships and better understand their implications.
